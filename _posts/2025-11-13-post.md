We had decided, prior to starting the track, that on the second day we would skip the next closest hut, Iris Burn, and soldier through to Motorau hut. About 30km in total.

By 7:30 am we were out the hut and on the track, the sun illuminating the surroundings in a golden morning glow. We clambered steadily up, the views somehow becoming more and more majestic as we did so. No-one else was on the track, we had the tops to ourselves!

It took us a few hours to pass the two shelters along the ridge, truly we were awe-struck by the scenery; plenty of photos were taken! Eventually though we had to descend (we finally ran into some other people at this point) and it was pretty relentless. The forest once again surrounded us, with snowy peaks poking out at us between gaps in canopy. We were followed by a kea for a little, squawking away in the tree-tops.

We reached the Iris Burn hut at 12, the perfect time for lunch. James' knee had just about held up this far, but it was not to last. A few hours into the next 15 km stint, it had completely gone. I found him a good walking stick and he persevered rather admirably. The forest was wonderful: myriad mosses and greens everywhere; interspersed with crystal clear rivers.

Motorau hut is perched on the shores of Lake Manapouri in the embrace of the mountains. We watched the sunset and got an early night, not before chatting with some Kiwi lawyers and hearing some anecdotes from Paul, the hut warden.
